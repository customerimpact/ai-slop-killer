## The 5 wins

### 1. Rewrite the subject line around one problem, and add preview text
**Now:** "Unlock Your Team's Full Potential 🚀"
**Why it costs you:** the subject line is the only part every person on the list sees. This one names no problem, no product and no result, so the inbox reads it as a promo and moves on. With no preview text set, the inbox shows "Hi there, In today's fast-paced digital landscape" under it, which makes it worse.
**Do this:** name the one task or cost FlowDesk removes, in the words a sales manager uses. Cut the emoji. Write preview text that adds the result or the number.
**Try:**
> Subject: [The one task FlowDesk removes from a sales rep's week]
> Preview: [What changes for the team, with the real number if you have one]

### 2. Open on one moment the reader knows, written for one reader
**Now:** "In today's fast-paced digital landscape, sales teams face more challenges than ever. That's where FlowDesk comes in."
**Why it costs you:** the first line decides whether the rest gets read. This one could open an email for any software, so the reader learns nothing about their own week. Later, "Whether you're a small startup or a growing enterprise" confirms it was written for nobody in particular.
**Do this:** delete both opening sentences. Pick the reader FlowDesk serves best and open on one situation from their week, in their words. Cut the "Whether you're" line.
**Try:**
> [One moment from a sales manager's week that FlowDesk fixes: the Monday pipeline call, the deal lost in someone's inbox, whatever your customers complain about most.]

### 3. Add one real proof point before the ask
**Now:** no customer, result or number in the email.
**Why it costs you:** the email asks a busy manager to give up time for a demo on the strength of "game-changer" and "transformative". With nothing to check, the safe choice is to ignore it. Proof is worth more here than any wording change.
**Do this:** add one line with a named customer and a result, or a plain count of teams using it. Put it right above the ask.
**Try:**
> [Customer name] at [company] [the result they got, with the number and time frame].

### 4. Say what FlowDesk is in one sentence, and turn the bullets into outcomes
**Now:** "FlowDesk isn't just another CRM. It's a game-changer that empowers your team to work smarter, not harder." and "Streamline your pipeline seamlessly / Leverage AI-powered insights to drive growth / Foster collaboration across your organization"
**Why it costs you:** the reader still can't say what FlowDesk does after reading the whole email. Any CRM could claim each of the three bullets, so none of them gives a reason to switch. The "cutting-edge platform... central hub" paragraph repeats the pitch without adding a fact.
**Do this:** write one plain sentence on what FlowDesk is and who it's for. Keep two bullets, each a feature plus what the reader stops doing or gets back. Delete the "cutting-edge platform" paragraph.
**Try:**
> FlowDesk is a CRM for [the sales teams you serve best]. It [the main job it does, in one plain sentence].
> - [Feature], so your reps [what they stop doing or get back]
> - [Feature], so you see [what a manager can now see, and when]

### 5. Make the ask say what happens on the demo, and sign it as a person
**Now:** "Ready to take your sales to the next level? Book a demo today." signed "The FlowDesk Team"
**Why it costs you:** "a demo" sounds like a sales call of unknown length, and a reader who's unsure what they get from it doesn't book. A team signature also gives the reader nobody to reply to.
**Do this:** drop the question. Say how long the demo is, what you'll show or build on the call, and any terms. Sign it with a real name and role.
**Try:**
> Book a [length]-minute demo. We'll [what happens on the call]. [Card or commitment terms.]
> [First name]
> [Role], FlowDesk
