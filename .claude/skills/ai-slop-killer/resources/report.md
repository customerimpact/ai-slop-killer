# The check report

## Shape

1. **The read line.** One sentence on what I read, the channel I took it to be, and its length. *"I read this as a LinkedIn post, about 110 words."* Never a question. If the user corrects the channel, I re-run.
2. **The score**, followed by one plain sentence on what the band means.
   - **0–19 clean:** reads like a person wrote it.
   - **20–44 noticeable:** a careful reader will spot a few patterns.
   - **45–69 heavy:** most readers will feel the template.
   - **70–100 saturated:** patterns in nearly every line.
3. **The three that matter most**, before anything else.
   - Ranked by what a reader feels first: openers and closers, then negation pivots and formulas, then structure, then vocabulary.
   - For each: the quoted line, why it reads as template, and a fixed version.
4. **Everything else**, grouped by line. One bullet each: the quote, then a short fix.
   - Vocabulary hits go together in one line: *"Swap these for plain words: leverage → use, seamless → (cut), ecosystem → your tools."*
5. **What the scanner can't see.** The judgment tells from `tells.md`, two to four at most, each with the quoted line and a fix.
6. **Kept as your voice**, only when a voice profile permitted a habit. One line: *"I left your em dashes alone; you use them in your own writing."*
7. **The close.** One line offering the fix: *"Want me to fix it? I'll ask you up to three quick questions so the new version has your real details in it."*

## Rules

- **Clean writing (score 0–19 with no high findings)** gets a short report: the score, any small notes, and "nothing worth fixing here". Never invent problems.
- **Name the pattern, never the author.**
- **Quote exactly**, keeping the user's spelling and punctuation.
- **Never list a rule ID or a severity.** Plain words only.
- **Long text (over about 1,500 words):** show the three that matter most and the counts per kind of pattern, then offer the full list.
