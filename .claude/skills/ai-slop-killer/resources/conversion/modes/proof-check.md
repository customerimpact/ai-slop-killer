# Proof check

A focused audit of every claim and every piece of proof, for a writer who suspects the page isn't believed.

## How to run it

1. **List the claims.** Every sentence that promises a result, states a fact about the product, or says something about customers. Quote each one.
2. **Match each claim to its proof.** For each claim, find the nearest support on the page: a testimonial, case study, number, screenshot description, logo, rating, guarantee, demo or credential. Read the matching guides in `resources/conversion/sections/` (testimonials, case-studies, data-and-research, proof-signals, endorsements, credibility, sample-and-demo, guarantee).
3. **Grade each claim:**
   - **Backed:** specific proof sits right next to it.
   - **Thin:** some support, but vague, far away or unverifiable ("Love it! – Sarah").
   - **Bare:** nothing on the page supports it.
4. **Check the proof itself:** specific (names, numbers, before and after), relevant to this reader, credible (full names, roles, photos, sources), placed where the doubt arises, and varied (not ten testimonials all saying "great").
5. **Score the Proof row** of the Copy Score as usual.

## Report format

```
I read this as a [copy type] for [reader]. I found [n] claims: [b] backed, [t] thin, [n] bare.

## Proof: n/20

| Claim | Grade | Nearest proof | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| "…" | Bare | none | add [what kind of proof] right after it |

**Strongest proof on the page:** quote it, and say where it should move to work harder.

**The biggest gap:** the one claim that most needs proof, and exactly what to collect (who to ask, what question to ask them, what number to pull).

**Proof to collect:** a short checklist of the proof the writer should gather, in priority order.
```
