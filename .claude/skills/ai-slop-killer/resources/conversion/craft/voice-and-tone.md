# Voice and tone

**Its job:** sound like one real, credible person or brand talking to one reader, in a register that fits the product, the audience and the moment.

**Applies to:** all copy. It matters most in long-form sales pages, emails and anything signed by a founder or coach, where a stiff or borrowed voice breaks trust.

## What good looks like
- The piece sounds like it came from one source, start to finish. Tone shifts are on purpose (warmer in the story, firmer at the offer).
- Register matches the buyer: plain and practical for busy owners, friendly and patient for beginners, direct for experts.
- It reads aloud well. Contractions, normal sentence length, mixed rhythm.
- Confidence comes from facts, not from volume. Claims are stated calmly.
- There's a point of view: the writer takes a side, admits a limit or says who it isn't for.
- If a voice profile exists, the copy matches its vocabulary, rhythm and habits.

## What usually goes wrong
- Generic, polished filler that could have come from any brand or any model.
- Hype: exclamation marks, all-caps words, "insane", "life-changing", fake urgency.
- Formal corporate voice on a product sold by a person (a coach who writes like a bank).
- Forced jokes or slang that don't fit the audience.
- The voice drifts: chatty intro, stiff feature list, pushy close.
- Talking down to the reader, or flattering them too hard.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** the voice actively hurts: hype, condescension, or a tone that clashes with the product and buyer.
- **1:** present but weak: inoffensive but generic, or uneven across sections.
- **2:** does its job: consistent, credible and fitting for this audience; reads naturally aloud.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: a distinct, likeable voice the reader would recognise without the logo, and that makes them trust the seller.

## Fixes to suggest
- Read the piece aloud. Rewrite every line you'd never say to a customer face to face.
- Remove exclamation marks and hype words, and replace them with one hard fact each.
- Pick the register for this buyer in one sentence, then edit every section to it.
- Add one honest admission: a limit, a trade-off, or who shouldn't buy.
- If the writer has samples of their own writing, match their word choices and sentence length.

## Example
Weak:
> Get ready to TRANSFORM your body!!! Ines Strength is the ULTIMATE program that will change your life forever!

Stronger:
> I'm Ines. Most of my clients sit at a desk all day, like you probably do.
> Three 40-minute sessions a week, for 12 weeks. You'll lift heavier, and your back will thank you.

The stronger one sounds like a coach talking to one person, and lets the plan carry the excitement.

**Facts only the writer has:** who's speaking, their real background, samples of how they write and talk, and any brand voice rules.
