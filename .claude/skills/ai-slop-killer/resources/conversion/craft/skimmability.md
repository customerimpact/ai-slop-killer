# Skimmability

**Its job:** let a reader who only scans the headings, bold lines, bullets and buttons still get the whole argument and find the action.

**Applies to:** all copy read on a screen. It matters most on long sales pages, landing pages and product pages, where most visitors skim before they decide to read.

## What good looks like
- Subheads carry the argument: read them alone, in order, and the sales case still makes sense.
- Paragraphs are short (one to three sentences on a screen), with the key point first.
- Bullets are used for lists that really are lists (features, what's included), each one starting with its strongest word.
- Bold is rare and marks the lines a skimmer must not miss.
- Key facts (price, trial, guarantee, delivery) are easy to spot, not buried in a paragraph.
- Calls to action are visually clear and repeated where a skimmer would stop.

## What usually goes wrong
- Walls of text with no breaks, especially on mobile.
- Label subheads ("Features", "About", "FAQ") that say nothing about the benefit.
- Everything bolded or highlighted, so nothing stands out.
- Bullet lists of ten or more near-identical items.
- The price or the main offer hidden in the middle of a long paragraph.
- Formatting that breaks the voice: fragments and emoji-style bullets everywhere.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** unscannable: dense blocks, no useful headings, key facts and the action hard to find.
- **1:** present but weak: some headings and bullets, but they're labels or filler, and the skim path misses the point.
- **2:** does its job: a skimmer gets the main benefit, the offer and the action from headings, bullets and bold alone.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the skim path is a complete, persuasive mini-version of the page that pulls the reader into the full text.

## Fixes to suggest
- Read only the subheads in order. Rewrite each as a benefit or a claim until they tell the story alone.
- Break paragraphs over three lines and move the key point to the front.
- Cut bullet lists to the five or six strongest items, and start each with the outcome.
- Limit bold to one line per section at most.
- Pull price, trial and guarantee into their own short block near each button.

## Example
Weak:
> **Features**
> Ledgerly has lots of features like invoicing and receipts and tax estimates and bank sync and reports, so you can manage everything in one place and save time every month.

Stronger:
> **Know your tax bill before it lands**
> - Snap a receipt, it's filed in seconds
> - Send an invoice from your phone
> - See your tax estimate update as you earn

The stronger one turns a label into a claim and gives skimmers three outcomes they can read at a glance.

**Facts only the writer has:** which features matter most to buyers, the real price and trial terms, and how the page is laid out on mobile.
