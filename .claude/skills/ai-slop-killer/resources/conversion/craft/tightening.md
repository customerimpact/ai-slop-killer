# Tightening

**Its job:** cut every word, line and section that doesn't move the reader closer to buying, so what's left reads fast and hits harder.

**Applies to:** all copy. It matters most in ads, subject lines, headlines, buttons and mobile pages, where space and attention are short. Long copy can still be tight.

## What good looks like
- Every sentence adds a fact, a reason, a proof point or a step. Nothing is there to fill space.
- Openings get to the point in the first line, with no warm-up.
- No repeated claims unless the repeat is a deliberate reminder near an ask.
- Words are short and active: "use" over "utilise", "we fixed" over "a fix was implemented".
- Qualifiers and hedges ("really", "very", "basically", "in order to") are gone.
- Length matches the decision: short for a cheap, simple buy, longer where the buyer needs more proof.

## What usually goes wrong
- Throat-clearing intros ("We're excited to share...", "Have you ever wondered...").
- The same benefit restated in three sections with new adjectives.
- Padding phrases: "the fact that", "at the end of the day", "when it comes to".
- Lists that keep going after the strong items, diluting them.
- Sections that exist because a template had them (mission statement, generic values).
- Cutting so hard that proof, specifics or objection handling disappear.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** bloated enough to lose the reader: filler, repetition and padding bury the offer.
- **1:** present but weak: the core is there, but intros, repeats and filler slow it down.
- **2:** does its job: little filler, fast openings, every section earns its place.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: nothing could be cut without losing a reason to buy, and the piece feels shorter than it is.

## Fixes to suggest
- Delete the first sentence of each section and see if it still works. It usually does.
- For every sentence, ask "would the reader miss this?" If not, cut it.
- Merge sections that make the same point, keeping the most specific version.
- Swap long phrases for short ones and passive verbs for active ones.
- Keep proof, price and objection answers even when cutting hard. Trim adjectives first.

## Example
Weak:
> We're really excited to let you know that, when it comes to great coffee, Harbor Roast is basically here to help you enjoy amazing coffee at home in order to start your day right.

Stronger:
> Fresh-roasted coffee at your door, from €14 a delivery.
> Skip or cancel any time.

The stronger one keeps the product, the price and the risk removal, and drops everything the reader would skip.

**Facts only the writer has:** which claims and proof points buyers respond to most, and any length limits for the channel (ad slots, subject lines, page layout).
