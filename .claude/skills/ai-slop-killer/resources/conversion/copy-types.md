# Copy types

To identify the type, check where the text lives and what it asks for: one long scroll ending in a price is a sales page, a single action with no navigation is a landing page or funnel step, a page with a cart button and specs is a product page, a form for payment details is an order page, a few lines with a link is an ad, a subject line plus body is an email, and spoken lines or timestamps mean a webinar or VSL script.

Section ids below refer to the guides in `resources/conversion/sections/`. "Needs" means score it; "can skip" means don't mark it down if it's absent.

## Long sales page

**Its one job:** take a reader from interested to bought on a single page, with no salesperson.

**Reader's state:** usually problem-aware or solution-aware. They know the pain, may have tried fixes, and don't yet know or trust this product. Often arriving from an email, ad or webinar.

**Needs:** headline, subheadline, opening, angle, problem, stakes, failed-fixes, mechanism, product-overview, features-and-outcomes, whats-inside, the-after, story, credibility, testimonials, case-studies, offer-stack, pricing, bonuses, guarantee, who-its-for, objections, faq, call-to-action, close, ps.

**Can skip:** tiers (unless there are several packages), comparison, data-and-research and endorsements if the proof is strong elsewhere, sample-and-demo for non-visual products, urgency if there's no real deadline.

**Typical length:** 2,000 to 8,000 words. Price and risk set the length: the more it costs, the more questions it has to answer.

**Most often missing:** the mechanism. Long pages describe the problem at length, then jump to the offer without a believable reason it works this time.

## Landing page

**Its one job:** get one action (start a trial, book a demo, buy one thing) from people who clicked a specific ad or link.

**Reader's state:** solution-aware or product-aware. They clicked a promise and want to confirm it fast.

**Needs:** headline, subheadline, opening (one or two lines), angle, problem (short), mechanism (short), how-it-works, product-overview, features-and-outcomes, proof-signals, testimonials, objections or faq, call-to-action.

**Can skip:** failed-fixes, story, offer-stack, bonuses, tiers, ps, long stakes. Pricing and guarantee only if the action is a purchase or trial with a cost.

**Typical length:** 300 to 1,500 words, with the call to action visible on the first screen and repeated.

**Most often missing:** message match. The headline doesn't repeat the promise of the ad that sent the traffic, so readers bounce before scrolling.

## Product page

**Its one job:** help a shopper who's already considering this product decide it's the right one and add it to the cart.

**Reader's state:** product-aware. They're comparing options, checking details and looking for reasons to trust it.

**Needs:** headline (product name plus the main benefit), subheadline, product-overview, features-and-outcomes, whats-inside, sample-and-demo (photos, video, screenshots), pricing, proof-signals, testimonials, guarantee, faq, call-to-action.

**Can skip:** opening, stakes, failed-fixes, story, offer-stack, bonuses, close, ps. Problem and mechanism can be one line each.

**Typical length:** 150 to 800 words, mostly scannable blocks.

**Most often missing:** plain facts. Pages lean on mood words and leave out size, format, delivery time, what's included and what happens if it doesn't fit.

## Opt-in or funnel step

**Its one job:** get a single small yes (an email address, a quiz start, a click to the next step) and set up the step that follows.

**Reader's state:** problem-aware, often cold, low trust and low patience. The ask is small, but so is their commitment.

**Needs:** headline, subheadline, angle, the-after (what the free thing gives them), whats-inside (what they'll get), proof-signals, call-to-action.

**Can skip:** almost everything else. Problem and opening in a line at most. No pricing, offer-stack, tiers, bonuses or long faq.

**Typical length:** 50 to 300 words.

**Most often missing:** a specific reason to give the email. "Join our newsletter" or "Get the free guide" without saying what the reader will be able to do afterwards.

## Order or checkout page

**Its one job:** keep the buyer's decision intact until payment goes through.

**Reader's state:** most-aware. They've decided. Anything that makes them think again risks the sale.

**Needs:** headline (confirming what they're getting), product-overview (short recap), pricing (total, billing frequency, taxes), guarantee, proof-signals (security, payment logos, a short testimonial), call-to-action.

**Can skip:** opening, angle, problem, stakes, failed-fixes, mechanism, story, case-studies, long faq, close, ps. Bonuses only as a reminder line. Urgency only if a real deadline was stated earlier.

**Typical length:** 50 to 250 words of copy around the form.

**Most often missing:** reassurance at the button: what happens after paying, when access or delivery starts, and how to cancel or get a refund.

## Paid social or search ad

**Its one job:** stop the right person and earn a click to the page, with a promise the page will keep.

**Reader's state:** social ads usually reach unaware or problem-aware people mid-scroll. Search ads reach solution-aware people who typed what they want.

**Needs:** headline (the hook), angle, problem or the-after (one of them, briefly), proof-signals (a number, a short quote), call-to-action. Search ads also need the searched term in the headline.

**Can skip:** nearly every long section: failed-fixes, mechanism (unless it's the hook), offer-stack, tiers, faq, guarantee, close, ps.

**Typical length:** search, a few short headlines and descriptions within the platform limits. Social, one to five sentences of primary text, sometimes a longer story post of 100 to 300 words.

**Most often missing:** one clear idea. Ads try to say everything, so the hook is weak and the click-through promise is fuzzy.

## Marketing email

**Its one job:** get the reader to click one link (or reply), or to take one step closer to buying.

**Reader's state:** depends on the list. Usually problem-aware or solution-aware subscribers who know the sender but aren't thinking about the offer.

**Needs:** headline (the subject line), subheadline (preview text), opening, angle, one of problem, story or the-after as the body, one real proof point before the ask (from testimonials, proof-signals or data-and-research), call-to-action. Sales emails in a launch also need pricing, urgency and guarantee mentions.

**Can skip:** offer-stack, tiers, full faq, case-studies, whats-inside detail. Leave the long sections to the page the email links to.

**Typical length:** 100 to 500 words. Story emails can run to 800.

**Most often missing:** a single clear ask. Emails end with several links or a soft "let me know your thoughts", so nobody knows what to do.

## Webinar or VSL script

**Its one job:** hold attention long enough to teach a new way to see the problem, then present the offer as the natural next step.

**Reader's state:** problem-aware or solution-aware, curious and willing to spend 15 to 90 minutes, but quick to leave if it drags.

**Needs:** headline (the opening line), opening, angle, problem, stakes, failed-fixes, mechanism, story, credibility, testimonials, case-studies, how-it-works, product-overview, offer-stack, pricing, bonuses, guarantee, urgency, who-its-for, objections, call-to-action, close.

**Can skip:** subheadline (folded into the first minute), tiers unless sold, comparison, faq (handled as spoken objections), ps.

**Typical length:** VSL, 1,500 to 6,000 spoken words (10 to 40 minutes). Webinar, 6,000 to 12,000 words (45 to 90 minutes), with the offer in the last third.

**Most often missing:** a clean turn into the offer. The teaching runs long, then the pitch arrives abruptly without tying the mechanism to what's for sale.
