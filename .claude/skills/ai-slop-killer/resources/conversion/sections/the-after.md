# The after

**Its job:** let the reader picture their life once the product works, so they want that future enough to act now.

**Needed on:** sales pages, landing pages, webinar and VSL scripts, usually after the solution and before the offer, or as the bridge into the call to action. Funnel steps and emails can use two or three lines of it. Product pages for simple goods can skip it.

## What good looks like
- It's specific and ordinary: a Tuesday morning, a moment at work, a conversation at home, not a fantasy.
- It mirrors the problem section. Each pain named earlier has a matching scene that's now different.
- It includes small, sensory details the reader recognises from their own life.
- The change is believable for this product and this time frame.
- It stays in the reader's voice and values. It shows what they actually want, not what the seller thinks they should want.
- It's tied back to the product, so the reader knows what made the change.

## What usually goes wrong
- Vague abstractions: "freedom", "peace of mind", "the life you deserve".
- Overreach: a pottery course that promises a new identity, an app that promises wealth.
- A future that doesn't match the earlier pain, so it feels pasted in.
- Every line is a cliché image (beach, laptop, sunrise).
- The section talks about the brand's vision instead of the reader's day.
- No link back to the product, so the reader can't see how they get there.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts.
- **1:** present but weak: abstract or clichéd, or promises more than the product can deliver.
- **2:** does its job: concrete scenes that mirror the problem and stay believable.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reader recognises the future as theirs and feels the cost of waiting.

## Fixes to suggest
- Pick one ordinary moment from the problem section and write the same moment, after.
- Replace each abstract noun ("confidence", "freedom") with what the reader would see or do.
- Check each claim against the product's real time frame and cut what it can't deliver.
- Add one small detail only a real customer would mention.
- End with a line that names the product as the bridge.

## Example
**Weak (Ines Strength):**
> Imagine a stronger, healthier, more confident you. Unlock the energy you deserve and live life to the fullest!

**Stronger (Ines Strength):**
> Twelve weeks from now, you carry the shopping up three floors and don't stop on the landing. Your back doesn't ache by 4 pm. You lift your kid onto your shoulders without thinking about it first. Three 40-minute sessions a week got you there.

Why it works: each scene is a moment a desk worker already dreads, now changed, with the effort stated.

**Facts only the writer has:** real customer moments and quotes, typical time to results, what the program can and can't change.
