# Product overview

**Its job:** say plainly what the product is, what's in it and what it costs in time and money, so the reader knows exactly what they're buying.

**Needed on:** every sales page, landing page and product page, usually after the mechanism or where the offer is introduced. Webinar and VSL scripts need it before the price. Order pages need a short recap. Ads and emails can keep it to a line, but it should never be missing from the page they link to.

## What good looks like
- One or two sentences a stranger could repeat: the format, the length, who it's for.
- Concrete facts: number of lessons or sessions, delivery format, what devices it works on, what's included.
- It says what the reader gets, not only what the product does.
- It's easy to find, with a clear heading or block, not scattered across the page.
- It matches the price and the offer section exactly. Nothing appears there that wasn't explained here.
- It names limits honestly (what it doesn't include) where that prevents a wrong purchase.

## What usually goes wrong
- The page describes benefits and feelings but never says what the product actually is.
- Abstract category words ("platform", "solution", "system") with no detail.
- Key facts like format, length or access only appear in the FAQ or the small print.
- Overloaded with every feature, so the core gets lost.
- Mismatch between what's described and what's in the checkout.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts (unclear or misleading about what's sold).
- **1:** present but weak: vague category language, or key facts missing or hidden.
- **2:** does its job: a plain description with the main facts, easy to find, consistent with the offer.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: so clear and concrete that the reader feels sure of what they'll get, and that certainty lowers the risk of buying.

## Fixes to suggest
- Write one sentence: "[Product] is a [format] that [does what] for [who]." Put it at the top of the section.
- List the hard facts: length, format, access, what's included, what it works with.
- Replace "platform" or "solution" with what the thing actually is.
- Move key facts out of the FAQ and into this section.
- Check every item here appears in the offer and the checkout, and the other way round.

## Example
Weak (Clay Weekends):
> Clay Weekends is a transformative creative journey that will awaken the artist within.

Stronger:
> Clay Weekends is an online course for complete beginners: 8 video lessons of about 25 minutes, built to be done over four weekends at your kitchen table. No wheel needed. €149, and you keep access for life.

The stronger one tells the reader the format, effort, equipment and price, so they know exactly what they're signing up for.

**Facts only the writer has:** the real lesson count and length, equipment list, and access terms.
