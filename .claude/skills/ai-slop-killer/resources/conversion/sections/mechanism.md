# Mechanism

**Its job:** explain the specific reason this product works when other approaches didn't, so the promise becomes believable.

**Needed on:** long sales pages and webinar or VSL scripts, after the problem and failed fixes. Landing pages for solution-aware readers need a short version. Product pages need at least one line on what makes it different. Ads can use a mechanism as the hook. Skip it on order pages, and for simple commodity products where the reader doesn't need a reason to believe.

## What good looks like
- It names one thing the product does differently and explains it in plain words.
- It connects directly to why the failed fixes failed. The missing piece there is the core here.
- A reader could explain it to a friend in a sentence.
- It's true and specific to the product. It describes how the product really works.
- It has a simple name or label if that helps people remember it, but the label is never a substitute for the explanation.
- It's backed by something: a demo, a number, a study, a before and after.

## What usually goes wrong
- No mechanism, so the promise rests on adjectives.
- Jargon or invented terms that sound impressive and explain nothing.
- A list of features presented as the mechanism, with no single reason it works.
- A mechanism that has nothing to do with the problem described earlier.
- Overclaiming: a "secret" or "breakthrough" that turns out to be ordinary.
- It's buried in the FAQ or never stated outright.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts (made up, misleading).
- **1:** present but weak: jargon, a feature list, or a fancy name without a real explanation.
- **2:** does its job: one clear reason it works, explained plainly and linked to the problem.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reader gets an "of course" moment and believes the result before seeing the proof.

## Fixes to suggest
- Finish this sentence: "It works because…" Use that as the core of the section.
- Tie it back to the failed fixes: "they miss X; we're built around X".
- Replace invented terms with what the thing actually does.
- Add one piece of evidence right after the explanation.
- If the product has many features, pick the one that makes the result happen and lead with it.

## Example
Weak (Harbor Roast):
> Our proprietary freshness technology delivers an elevated coffee experience.

Stronger:
> We roast on Monday and ship on Tuesday, sized so each bag runs out just as the next one arrives. You never drink coffee older than three weeks, and you never run out.

The stronger one explains how the freshness and the convenience actually happen, so the reader can believe both.

**Facts only the writer has:** the real roast and shipping schedule, bag sizes, and how delivery timing is set.
