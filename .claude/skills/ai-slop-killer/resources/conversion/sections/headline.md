# Headline

**Its job:** make the right reader stop and believe the next line is worth their time, by naming a result they want or a problem they have.

**Needed on:** every copy type. On a sales page, landing page or product page it's the first big line on the screen. In an ad it's the hook or the first line of text. In an email the subject line does this job and the first line backs it up. In a webinar or VSL script it's the first sentence spoken. Never skip it.

## What good looks like
- It names a specific outcome, problem or reader. You can tell who it's for without reading anything else.
- It holds one idea. A reader can repeat it after one glance.
- It matches the promise in the ad, link or email that brought the reader here.
- It contains a detail a competitor couldn't copy word for word: a number, a time frame, a situation, a mechanism.
- It's believable for this reader at this level of awareness. Big claims come with a hint of why they're true.
- It reads like a person talking, not a slogan.

## What usually goes wrong
- A brand line or tagline ("Bookkeeping, reimagined") that says nothing about the reader.
- A clever pun or wordplay that makes the reader decode it.
- Two or three ideas joined with commas or "and", so nothing lands.
- A claim so large or vague that the reader discounts it on sight.
- It doesn't match the traffic source, so the reader wonders if they're on the wrong page.
- It talks about the product's category ("All-in-one scheduling platform") instead of what changes for the reader.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts.
- **1:** present but weak: generic, clever-but-empty, a brand slogan, or a claim with nothing specific in it.
- **2:** does its job: names a clear outcome or problem for a clear reader, one idea, matches the traffic source.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: specific, believable, hard to copy, and it makes the right reader feel the page was written about them.

## Fixes to suggest
- Write down the one result the reader wants most, then put it in the headline in their words.
- Add one concrete detail: a number, a time frame, the reader's situation or the name of the method.
- Cut any second idea and move it to the subheadline.
- Check the ad or email that sends traffic here and use the same promise.
- Read it aloud to someone in the audience. If they ask "meaning what?", rewrite.

## Example
Weak (Ledgerly):
> Smarter finances for the modern freelancer.

Stronger:
> Your books done in 10 minutes a week, so tax season stops eating your January.

The stronger one names a measurable result and the pain it removes, in words a freelancer would use.

**Facts only the writer has:** the real time saved, the actual customer quote or phrasing, and the promise used in the ads that send traffic.
