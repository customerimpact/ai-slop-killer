# Pricing and price framing

**Its job:** state the price clearly and set it against something that makes it feel fair, so the reader judges it on value rather than flinching at the number.

**Needed on:** sales pages, product pages, pricing pages and checkout steps, after the value has been built. Webinar and VSL scripts need it before the ask. Ads and emails can mention price when it's a selling point, or leave it to the page.

## What good looks like
- The price is easy to find and unambiguous: amount, currency, billing period, what's included, tax if relevant.
- It's framed against a real anchor: the cost of the problem, the cost of the alternative, or a per-day or per-use breakdown that's honest.
- Recurring costs say so plainly, with how to cancel.
- Payment options (monthly, yearly, instalments) are shown with the total for each.
- It appears after the reader understands what they're getting, not before.

## What usually goes wrong
- The price is hidden, vague ("affordable") or only revealed at checkout.
- Fake anchors: a crossed-out "regular price" that was never charged.
- Per-day framing that hides a big total or a long commitment.
- Surprise fees, auto-renewals or tax that only appear at the last step.
- Apologising for the price, which tells the reader it's too high.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** price missing where the reader needs it, or framed in a misleading way (fake discount, hidden renewal).
- **1:** present but weak: price stated with no frame, or a frame that doesn't hold up.
- **2:** does its job: clear, complete, honest, with a sensible comparison.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the anchor is specific to this buyer's situation, so the price reads as an obvious trade.

## Fixes to suggest
- Put the full price, billing period and cancellation terms in one place, next to the button.
- Anchor against what the problem costs the reader now, using a number they recognise.
- Remove any "was" price you can't prove was charged.
- If there's an instalment plan, show its total beside the one-off price.
- State the price once with confidence. Cut any wording that sounds like an apology.

## Example
**Weak (Ines Strength):**
> Normally €999, today just €390! That's less than a coffee a day!

**Stronger (Ines Strength):**
> The program is €390 for 12 weeks, paid once, with Ines reviewing your form every week. That's about the price of six private sessions at a typical gym, for three months of coaching. Prefer to spread it? Three payments of €135 (€405 total).

Why it works: the price is complete, the anchor is a real alternative the reader knows, and the instalment cost is shown, not hidden.

**Facts only the writer has:** exact prices, billing terms, instalment totals, any real past price, and local costs of the alternative used as the anchor.
