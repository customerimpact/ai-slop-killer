# What's inside

**Its job:** make the reader see exactly what they get when they pay, so the offer feels real, complete and bigger than the price.

**Needed on:** sales pages and product pages for courses, programs, memberships, bundles and services, just before or inside the offer. Landing pages need a compact version. Software can often swap this for features and outcomes. Ads and emails can skip it or name one standout item.

## What good looks like
- Every module, component or deliverable is named and paired with what the reader can do after it.
- The format is clear: videos, lessons, calls, templates, physical items, how many, how long, how delivered.
- The order follows the buyer's path (week 1, week 2...) so they can picture working through it.
- There's a visual where it helps: a product shot, a module grid, a screenshot of the member area.
- Bonuses are listed separately, each with its own reason to exist, not padding.
- Access terms are stated plainly: how long, on which devices, what happens after the program ends.

## What usually goes wrong
- A table of contents with chapter titles and nothing else ("Module 3: Foundations").
- Cute module names that hide what's taught.
- Bulk as the selling point: "47 videos, 300 pages" with no word on what any of it does.
- Bonuses that look invented to inflate value, each with an unexplained price tag.
- Missing basics: no word on length, format or how the reader gets it, so they hesitate.
- The section is so long that the reader loses track of the offer.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts.
- **1:** present but weak: a list of names or counts with no result attached, or unclear format and access.
- **2:** does its job: each part is named, explained in one line, in buyer order, with format and access stated.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reader can picture finishing each part and what they'll have in hand, and the whole feels like more than the price.

## Fixes to suggest
- Rewrite each module title as "what you'll be able to do after it".
- Add format and length to every item (for example "4 videos, 35 minutes, one worksheet").
- Show the member area or the physical box. A screenshot beats a paragraph.
- Keep only bonuses that solve a problem the reader will hit. Say what that problem is.
- State access terms in one sentence near the price.

## Example
**Weak (Clay Weekends):**
> Module 1: Getting Started. Module 2: The Wheel. Module 3: Glazing. Plus bonus content and lifetime access!

**Stronger (Clay Weekends):**
> **Weekend 1: your first three bowls.** Centre clay and pull walls that stay up (5 videos, 50 min).
> **Weekend 2: mugs with handles that don't crack.** Trimming, attaching, drying (6 videos, 1 h).
> **Weekend 3: glaze without a studio kiln.** Which local kilns fire for beginners and how to ask.
> Lifetime access on phone, tablet or laptop.

Why it works: each weekend ends with an object the reader can picture on their shelf.

**Facts only the writer has:** real module list, video counts and lengths, bonus contents, access terms, delivery method.
