# Credibility

**Its job:** answer "why should I trust this seller?" so the reader believes the person or company can deliver what the page promises.

**Needed on:** sales pages, landing pages, webinar and VSL scripts, and any page where the buyer depends on the seller's skill (coaching, courses, services, B2B software). Often an "about the coach" or "who we are" block near the middle, plus a line early on. Product pages for well-known goods can keep it brief. Ads and emails need one line at most.

## What good looks like
- It names relevant experience: what the seller has done that matches the reader's problem.
- Facts beat adjectives: years, number of clients, qualifications, results, where they've worked.
- It shows the seller understands the reader's situation, ideally because they lived it.
- It includes a face, a real name and a way to check (a profile, an address, a registration number).
- It admits limits or who the product isn't for, which makes the rest more believable.
- It's short and placed where doubt appears, not saved for the bottom.

## What usually goes wrong
- Self-praise with no facts ("world-class experts", "passionate team").
- Credentials that don't relate to the reader's problem.
- An anonymous brand with no people, address or way to contact anyone.
- A long biography that pushes the offer off the screen.
- Claims that can't be checked, or numbers that look rounded up.
- Credibility hidden on an "about" page the reader never visits.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts.
- **1:** present but weak: adjectives and vague claims, or credentials unrelated to the problem.
- **2:** does its job: relevant, checkable facts about a real person or team, placed where the reader needs them.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reader feels this seller is the obvious person to solve their exact problem.

## Fixes to suggest
- Swap every adjective about the seller for a fact that proves it.
- Keep only credentials that relate to the reader's problem. Drop the rest.
- Add a photo, a full name and one way to check.
- Add a line on who this isn't for.
- Move a short credibility line up next to the first big claim.

## Example
**Weak (Ines Strength):**
> Ines is a passionate, highly experienced fitness expert dedicated to helping you achieve your goals.

**Stronger (Ines Strength):**
> I'm Ines. I worked at a desk for nine years and hurt my back lifting a suitcase. Since retraining as a strength coach I've coached 600 office workers, most of them over 35. This program isn't for athletes or anyone with an injury that's still being treated.

Why it works: relevant facts, a shared problem and an honest limit, all checkable.

**Facts only the writer has:** qualifications, years, client counts, where the seller has worked, registration details, a real photo.
