# PS

**Its job:** catch the skimmer who jumps to the end, and give them the one reason and one action that might make them buy.

**Needed on:** sales letters and long sales pages, at the very end after the sign-off. Optional on marketing emails, where it can carry the link a second time. Webinar and VSL scripts can use a spoken version. Landing pages, product pages and ads skip it. Skip it in any format where it would look odd.

## What good looks like
- It stands alone: someone who reads only the PS knows what's offered and what to do.
- It carries one strong point: the main result, the guarantee, a real deadline, or a short proof.
- It's short, usually two to four lines.
- It includes or points to the link or button.
- A second PS, if used, adds something new instead of repeating the first.

## What usually goes wrong
- Repeating the last paragraph word for word.
- Introducing a brand-new claim or bonus that needed explaining earlier.
- No link, so the skimmer has nowhere to go.
- A chain of PSs that reads like a pile of afterthoughts.
- Fake urgency dropped in at the end because it "always works".

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** misleading (fake deadline, new unexplained promise), or missing where the format clearly calls for one.
- **1:** present but weak: repeats the body, no link, or makes no point.
- **2:** does its job: short, stands alone, one strong point, a link.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: a skimmer reading only the headline and the PS would understand the offer and want to click.

## Fixes to suggest
- Write the PS for a reader who skipped everything else: offer, main reason, action.
- Pick the single strongest point (often the guarantee or the deadline) and lead with it.
- Add the link or button text inside the PS.
- Cut any second PS that only repeats the first.
- Check any deadline or number in the PS matches the body exactly.

## Example
**Weak (Clay Weekends):**
> PS: Don't forget, this is an amazing course and you'll love it! Sign up now!

**Stronger (Clay Weekends):**
> PS: If you've wanted to try the wheel but worried you'd waste money on a class you'd quit, this costs €149 once and you keep it for life. Start with lesson one this weekend: [Join Clay Weekends].

Why it works: a skimmer gets the offer, the price, the risk answer and the link in two lines.

**Facts only the writer has:** price, access terms, guarantee, any real deadline, and the link.
