# Comparison

**Its job:** show the reader, fairly and specifically, why this option beats what they'd otherwise do, so they stop shopping around.

**Needed on:** sales pages and product pages in crowded markets, usually after the offer is explained and before pricing. Useful in funnel steps and demo pages where buyers are clearly weighing options. Skip it in short ads and most emails, or when the reader isn't comparing anything yet.

## What good looks like
- It compares against what the reader actually considers: a named category, a spreadsheet, doing it themselves, hiring someone, or doing nothing.
- Each row or point is a difference the buyer cares about (time, cost, effort, risk), stated in concrete terms.
- It admits where the alternative is fine or better, and says who that alternative suits.
- Claims about the other side are checkable and current. Nothing a competitor could call false.
- It ends by pointing at the choice: if these differences matter to you, here's the next step.

## What usually goes wrong
- A table of ticks for us and crosses for "them", where "them" is a vague strawman nobody believes.
- Feature rows the buyer doesn't care about, picked only because the product wins them.
- No mention of the cheapest alternative: doing nothing or using what they already have.
- Unverifiable or outdated claims about competitors, which hurt trust and can create legal risk.
- Comparing on price alone, which invites a race to the bottom.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where buyers are clearly comparing, or it's a dishonest strawman that costs trust.
- **1:** present but weak: generic tick-box table, vague "other tools", no reason given.
- **2:** does its job: real alternatives, differences that matter, stated plainly and fairly.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: concedes what's fair, quantifies the gap, and makes the reader feel the cost of the alternative they were leaning toward.

## Fixes to suggest
- List what your buyers actually use today, then compare against those two or three options only.
- Replace each tick with the outcome behind it ("export ready for your accountant in one click").
- Add one honest line on who the alternative suits better.
- Include "keep doing it yourself" as a column, with its real cost in hours or mistakes.
- Cut any claim about a competitor you can't back with a source and a date.

## Example
**Weak (Ledgerly):**
> Ledgerly vs other apps: ✓ Easy ✓ Fast ✓ Affordable. Others: ✗ Complicated ✗ Slow ✗ Expensive.

**Stronger (Ledgerly):**
> Most freelancers we talk to use a spreadsheet. It's free and fine for ten invoices a year. Past that, you're matching payments by hand each quarter, roughly a day of work. Ledgerly matches them as they land, for €19 a month. If you send fewer than one invoice a month, keep the spreadsheet.

Why it works: it names the real alternative, prices the hidden cost, and its honesty about who shouldn't switch makes the rest believable.

**Facts only the writer has:** what buyers currently use, measured time or cost of each alternative, verified competitor pricing and features with dates.
