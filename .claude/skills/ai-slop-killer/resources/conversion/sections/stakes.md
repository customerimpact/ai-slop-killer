# Stakes

**Its job:** show the reader what keeps happening if they do nothing, so that staying put feels like a choice with a price.

**Needed on:** long sales pages and webinar or VSL scripts, right after the problem. Landing pages and emails need a sentence or two, often folded into the problem. Ads can use it as the hook. Product pages and order pages rarely need it. Skip it for low-cost, low-risk purchases where heavy stakes would feel out of proportion.

## What good looks like
- It puts a number or a concrete picture on the cost of waiting: money, hours, missed chances, health, stress.
- It's grounded in the reader's real situation, not a doom scenario.
- It's proportionate to the price and the problem. A €14 coffee subscription doesn't need stakes about a ruined life.
- It looks ahead: what the next month, quarter or year looks like if nothing changes.
- It's honest and can be backed up.
- It moves quickly to hope. The reader leaves this section wanting the fix, not feeling crushed.

## What usually goes wrong
- No stakes at all, so the reader agrees there's a problem and decides to deal with it later.
- Fear-mongering or exaggerated outcomes that break trust.
- Vague stakes ("you'll fall behind") with no measure of what that means.
- Stakes that don't match the product's size or price.
- It goes on too long and the page feels like a lecture.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts (scare tactics, false claims).
- **1:** present but weak: vague, generic, or out of proportion.
- **2:** does its job: a clear, believable cost of waiting that fits this reader and this price.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the cost of doing nothing is so concrete that the price starts to look small.

## Fixes to suggest
- Work out the cost of the problem over a month or a year and show it plainly, if you have real figures.
- Replace "fall behind" style phrases with one concrete future moment.
- Scale the stakes to the price. Cut anything that sounds dramatic for the size of the purchase.
- End the section with a turn toward the solution.
- Check every claim can be supported. Remove any that can't.

## Example
Weak (Ledgerly):
> If you don't get organised, things could get really bad for your business.

Stronger:
> Every receipt you don't log is a deduction you'll probably forget. At €40 of missed costs a month, that's close to €500 a year handed back to the tax office, plus the weekend you lose sorting it in April.

The stronger one gives the cost of waiting in money and time the reader can picture, and it's bigger than the price.

**Facts only the writer has:** real figures on missed deductions or time lost, from customer data or a source the writer can cite.
