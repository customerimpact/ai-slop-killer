# Failed fixes

**Its job:** name what the reader has already tried and explain why it didn't work, so they stop blaming themselves and become open to something different.

**Needed on:** long sales pages and webinar or VSL scripts, between the problem and the mechanism. Landing pages and emails aimed at solution-aware readers benefit from a short version. Skip it for readers who've never tried anything (first purchase in a new category) or on order pages and short product pages.

## What good looks like
- It lists the real things this reader has tried: other apps, spreadsheets, gym memberships, video tutorials, doing it themselves.
- It explains the reason each one fails, in a way that sets up the mechanism.
- It's fair. It names categories of approach, not named rivals, and doesn't mock them.
- It moves the blame from the reader to the method ("it wasn't you, the approach was missing X").
- It's brief. Two to four failed fixes, a line or two each.
- The reasons ring true to someone who tried them.

## What usually goes wrong
- It's missing, so the reader assumes this is one more of the things they've tried.
- It sneers at competitors or the reader's past choices.
- It lists fixes the reader never tried.
- It says other methods "don't work" without saying why.
- The reasons given have nothing to do with what this product does differently.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts (attacks rivals, insults the reader).
- **1:** present but weak: lists alternatives without real reasons, or picks ones the reader didn't try.
- **2:** does its job: names the fixes the reader tried, gives a fair reason each fell short, and sets up the mechanism.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reader feels relieved that it wasn't their fault, and the missing piece points straight at this product.

## Fixes to suggest
- Ask customers what they used before and list the top two or three.
- For each, write one honest sentence on why it falls short for this reader.
- Make the shared reason they fail the exact thing the mechanism fixes.
- Remove any named rival and any mocking tone.
- Add a line that takes the blame off the reader.

## Example
Weak (Ines Strength):
> Other programs are bad. Ours is better.

Stronger:
> You've tried the free apps and the January gym membership. They assume you can train like someone who isn't stiff from eight hours in a chair. When your back complains in week two, you stop. That isn't a willpower problem.

The stronger one names fixes the reader recognises, gives a fair reason they failed and points to what the program does differently.

**Facts only the writer has:** what customers actually tried before and why they say it didn't work, from surveys or intake forms.
