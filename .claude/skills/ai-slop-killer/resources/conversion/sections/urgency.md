# Urgency and scarcity: honest only

**Its job:** give the reader a true reason to decide now rather than someday.

**Needed on:** launch sales pages, webinar and VSL scripts, and launch or reminder emails, near the price and the final ask. Evergreen product pages and most subscriptions don't need artificial urgency; the cost of waiting can do the job. Skip it whenever there's no real deadline or limit.

## What good looks like
- Every deadline or limit is real: a date the cart closes, a cohort size, seasonal stock, a price change that will happen.
- The reason for the limit is explained (coaching capacity, batch size, start date).
- The exact date, time and time zone are given, or the exact number left.
- Where no deadline exists, it points to the real cost of waiting: another month of the problem.
- The deadline is kept. After it passes, the page changes.

## What usually goes wrong
- Countdown timers that reset on reload or restart for every visitor.
- "Only 3 spots left" on something with no limit.
- Pressure words ("act now", "last chance") with no date or reason behind them.
- Deadlines that are extended again and again, teaching the list to ignore them.
- Urgency so loud it makes the offer look desperate.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** fake urgency (resetting timers, invented scarcity), or none on a launch that has a real close date.
- **1:** present but weak: pressure words with no date, number or reason.
- **2:** does its job: a real deadline or limit, stated exactly, with a reason.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reason for the limit is part of the value (small cohort, personal coaching), and the cost of waiting is concrete for this reader.

## Fixes to suggest
- Replace every vague pressure phrase with a date, a number, or nothing.
- Say why the limit exists, in one line.
- If there's no real deadline, cut the timer and write about what another month of the problem costs.
- Remove any counter or timer that isn't wired to a real limit.
- Plan what the page and emails say after the deadline, and keep to it.

## Example
**Weak (Ines Strength):**
> HURRY! Spots are filling fast! Don't miss out, this offer won't last!

**Stronger (Ines Strength):**
> The next group starts on Monday 12 January. Ines reviews every lifter's form herself each week, so she takes 25 people per group. Enrolment closes on Thursday 8 January at 20:00 CET, or when the 25th place is taken.

Why it works: the date and number are exact, and the reason for the cap (personal form reviews) is itself a selling point.

**Facts only the writer has:** real close dates and times, cohort or stock limits, the reason for the limit, and what happens after the deadline.
