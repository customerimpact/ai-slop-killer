# Call to action: buttons, links, the ask

**Its job:** tell the reader exactly what to do next and what happens when they do, so taking the step feels easy and safe.

**Needed on:** every copy type. Sales and landing pages need it after the offer and repeated at natural decision points. Product pages need it near the price. Ads, emails and funnel steps need one clear ask. Never skip it.

## What good looks like
- One main action per page or email. Secondary actions are visibly secondary.
- Button text says what the reader gets or does ("Start my 14-day trial"), not "Submit".
- The lines next to the button answer last-second worries: price, card needed or not, cancel terms, what happens next.
- The ask is repeated where readers are likely to be ready, not only at the very bottom.
- The text matches the destination. What the button promises is what the next screen delivers.

## What usually goes wrong
- Generic buttons ("Learn more", "Click here", "Submit") that hide the outcome.
- Several competing asks of equal weight (buy, book, subscribe, follow).
- Buttons with no nearby price or terms, so the click feels like a leap.
- The ask buried at the end of long copy with nothing earlier.
- A button promise ("Get instant access") that leads to a long form or upsell.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** no clear ask, or several competing ones, or a misleading button.
- **1:** present but weak: generic button text, no reassurance nearby, only at the end.
- **2:** does its job: one clear action, specific button text, key terms beside it, repeated sensibly.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the ask restates the main result in the reader's words, and the nearby lines remove every remaining fear about clicking.

## Fixes to suggest
- Rewrite the button as "verb + what they get", ideally in the first person ("Book my demo").
- Pick one main action and demote or remove the others.
- Add one line under the button covering price, card, cancel terms or what happens next.
- Place the ask after the offer, after the guarantee and at the end, at minimum.
- Click through yourself and check the next screen keeps the button's promise.

## Example
**Weak (ShiftDesk):**
> [Learn more] [Contact us] [Subscribe to newsletter]

**Stronger (ShiftDesk):**
> [Book a 20-minute demo]
> We'll build next week's rota for one of your sites, live on the call. No commitment, no card.

Why it works: one ask, a button that says what happens, and a line that shows the reader what they'll get from the call before they book.

**Facts only the writer has:** what actually happens after the click, demo length, trial and card terms, price shown at that step.
