# Objections handled in the body

**Its job:** answer the doubts that stop people buying at the moment they arise, inside the main copy, so they never pile up.

**Needed on:** sales pages, webinar and VSL scripts, longer landing pages, and nurture emails, woven through the body wherever a doubt naturally comes up. Short ads can handle one objection at most. Skip a separate block only if the body already handles them all.

## What good looks like
- The main objections are known (price, time, "will it work for me", switching cost, trust) and each one is met.
- Each answer comes right after the claim that would trigger the doubt, not saved for the end.
- The objection is named in the reader's own words before it's answered.
- Answers use evidence: a fact, a mechanism, a result, a policy. Not reassurance alone.
- Some objections are conceded honestly and then put in proportion.

## What usually goes wrong
- Only easy objections are answered, while the real one (usually price or "will it work for me") is dodged.
- Answers that just repeat the claim louder.
- All objections pushed to an FAQ at the bottom that most readers never reach.
- Strawman objections set up only to be knocked down.
- Dismissive tone that makes the reader feel silly for doubting.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** the main objection is ignored, or answers are dismissive.
- **1:** present but weak: easy doubts answered, the big one dodged, or reassurance without evidence.
- **2:** does its job: the main objections named and answered with evidence, near where they arise.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reader feels their exact doubt was anticipated, and the answer turns it into a reason to go ahead.

## Fixes to suggest
- Collect the top objections from sales calls, refund reasons and replies, and rank them.
- After each big claim, ask what a sceptic would say next, and answer that in the next lines.
- Phrase each objection the way buyers say it, then answer with a fact, a proof point or a policy.
- Concede what's true ("yes, it takes time") and show why it's still worth it.

## Example
**Weak (ShiftDesk):**
> Worried about switching? Don't be! ShiftDesk is super easy and your team will love it.

**Stronger (ShiftDesk):**
> Your managers have built rotas in spreadsheets for years, and nobody wants a messy changeover in the middle of service. So we import your current rota, set up your sites with you on one call, and run both side by side for the first two weeks. Staff only need the app to see and swap shifts.

Why it works: it names the real fear in the owner's language and answers it with specific steps, not cheerleading.

**Facts only the writer has:** the actual objections buyers raise, onboarding steps and timing, proof points and policies that answer them.
