# Data and research

**Its job:** back the page's key claims with numbers or studies the reader can check, so belief rests on evidence rather than the seller's word.

**Needed on:** sales pages, landing pages, product pages and webinar or VSL scripts whose claims are measurable (health, finance, productivity, B2B results). Place each figure next to the claim it supports. Ads and emails can use one figure with a source. Skip it when the offer sells on taste or experience and no claim needs proof.

## What good looks like
- Each number supports a specific claim and sits right next to it.
- Sources are named and dated, with a link or footnote the reader can follow.
- Own data is labelled as own data: how many customers, over what period, how measured.
- Numbers are precise where the data is precise ("37%", not "nearly 40%") and honest about limits.
- One or two strong figures carry the section. Each figure is explained in plain words.
- Charts, if used, are simple and readable on a phone.

## What usually goes wrong
- "Studies show" or "research proves" with no source.
- Statistics about the whole market that say nothing about this product.
- Old, unsourced or rounded-up numbers that look made up.
- A wall of figures with no explanation of what they mean for the reader.
- Correlation sold as cause, or one small study sold as settled science.
- Own results presented as independent research.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts (for example invented or misleading numbers).
- **1:** present but weak: unsourced, irrelevant or confusing figures.
- **2:** does its job: relevant, sourced figures placed next to the claims they support, explained plainly.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the evidence makes the main promise feel likely for this reader, and it's easy to check.

## Fixes to suggest
- Add the source, date and sample size to every figure, or cut it.
- Replace market statistics with the product's own measured results where you have them.
- Write one plain sentence under each figure saying what it means for the reader.
- Cut to the strongest two or three figures.
- Label own data clearly ("Ledgerly users, Jan to Jun, 2,100 accounts").

## Example
**Weak (Ines Strength):**
> Studies show strength training is the best way to fix back pain. Science proves it works!

**Stronger (Ines Strength):**
> Of 212 clients who finished the program last year, 148 (70%) rated their back pain lower at week 12 than at week 1, on a 0–10 scale they filled in themselves.
> This is our own client survey, not a clinical trial.

Why it works: a specific, honest figure with sample, measure and limits stated, tied to the main promise.

**Facts only the writer has:** real figures, sample sizes, dates, how they were measured, the sources and links.
