# Who it's for and who it isn't

**Its job:** let the right reader recognise themselves and the wrong reader leave, so the buyers who stay feel this was made for them.

**Needed on:** sales pages, landing pages, webinar and VSL scripts, and higher-priced product pages, usually after the offer is clear and before the price, or near the FAQ. Ads can do it in the targeting line. Skip it on low-cost impulse products where fit is obvious.

## What good looks like
- "For you if" points describe situations, stages or habits, not flattering traits anyone would claim.
- "Not for you if" points are real exclusions that cost the seller some buyers.
- The criteria match the people who actually get results, based on past buyers.
- It's short: a few points on each side, each one a single clear sentence.
- The exclusions make the inclusions more believable.

## What usually goes wrong
- "This is for anyone who wants to succeed", which excludes no one and convinces no one.
- "Not for you if you're lazy" style lines that insult instead of filter.
- Criteria too broad to help a reader decide.
- No "not for" at all, so the section reads as a second benefit list.
- Exclusions that contradict claims made elsewhere on the page.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where fit matters (high price, real effort required), or it insults the reader.
- **1:** present but weak: flattering generic traits, no real exclusions.
- **2:** does its job: specific situations on both sides, consistent with the rest of the page.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the right reader feels described exactly, and the honest exclusions make them trust everything else.

## Fixes to suggest
- Write the "for" list from your best past buyers' starting point: what they had, did and struggled with.
- Add at least two "not for" points you'd honestly turn people away for.
- Replace traits ("motivated", "ambitious") with situations ("you can give it three evenings a week").
- Check every exclusion against the claims and guarantee elsewhere on the page.

## Example
**Weak (Clay Weekends):**
> Perfect for anyone who loves creativity and wants to try something new!

**Stronger (Clay Weekends):**
> For you if you've never touched a wheel, can rent or borrow one, and have two free weekends a month.
> Not for you if you want to hand-build without a wheel (we don't cover it), or you already throw and want advanced glazing.

Why it works: a real beginner sees their exact situation, and the honest exclusions save the wrong buyer a refund.

**Facts only the writer has:** who actually succeeds with the product, the real requirements (equipment, time, level), and what the product doesn't cover.
