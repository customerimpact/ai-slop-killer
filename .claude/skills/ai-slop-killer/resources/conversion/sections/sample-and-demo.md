# Sample or demo

**Its job:** let the reader try or see the product working, so belief comes from their own eyes instead of the seller's claims.

**Needed on:** sales, landing and product pages for software, courses, food and drink, and anything whose quality is hard to describe. Sits after the main claims, or next to the call to action as a lower-commitment step. Webinar or VSL scripts often are the demo. Skip it when the product is a commodity the reader already understands.

## What good looks like
- The reader sees the product doing the one thing that matters most, early in the demo.
- The sample is real: an actual lesson, a real screen, a real tasting note, not a polished trailer.
- It's short and easy to reach: a 60–90 second video, a free lesson, a live sandbox, a sample box.
- The demo uses a situation the reader recognises, with realistic data or a realistic customer.
- There's a clear next step right after it (start the trial, book the full demo, order the box).
- Any form or gate in front of it asks only for what's needed.

## What usually goes wrong
- A brand video with music and smiling people instead of the product in use.
- A demo that tours every menu before showing the result the reader came for.
- A "free sample" that is really a long sales pitch.
- A demo gated behind a long form or a sales call for a cheap product.
- No call to action after the demo, so interest fades.
- Fake-looking data or a perfect setup the reader knows their own life won't match.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts.
- **1:** present but weak: a trailer or tour that shows little of the real product, or hard to reach.
- **2:** does its job: a real look at the core result, short, easy to access, with a clear next step.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reader gets a small real win or sees their own case solved, and wants the rest.

## Fixes to suggest
- Open the demo on the result, then show how it got there.
- Replace the trailer with a raw screen recording or one full free lesson.
- Use the reader's typical case as the demo scenario.
- Cut the gate to an email or nothing. Save qualifying questions for after.
- Put the main call to action directly under the demo, repeating what they just saw.

## Example
**Weak (ShiftDesk):**
> Watch our video to see how ShiftDesk is transforming restaurant scheduling. [3-minute brand film]

**Stronger (ShiftDesk):**
> See a 4-location rota built in 90 seconds. A manager drags in next week's bookings, ShiftDesk fills the shifts, and two staff swap a Friday from their phones.
> [Play the 90-second demo] Then book a 20-minute demo with your own locations.

Why it works: the reader sees their own Monday problem solved before they're asked for anything.

**Facts only the writer has:** what the demo actually shows, real timings, what's in a free lesson or sample box, gating rules.
