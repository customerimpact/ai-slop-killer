# Testimonials

**Its job:** let real customers say what the seller can't credibly say about themselves, so the reader believes the result will happen for them too.

**Needed on:** sales pages, landing pages, product pages and funnel steps, placed near the claims they support and close to the call to action. Ads can carry one short quote. Marketing emails can use one or two. Webinar and VSL scripts read them aloud or show them on screen.

## What good looks like
- Each quote is specific: a result, a number, a before and after, or an objection that was overcome.
- The person is identifiable: full name or first name and city, role, a photo, ideally a link or video.
- The people quoted look like the target reader, so the reader thinks "that's me".
- Quotes sit next to the claim they prove, not all in one block at the bottom.
- The customer's own words are kept, including rough edges, so they sound real.
- A mix covers the main doubts: price, time, "will it work for me", support.

## What usually goes wrong
- Vague praise: "Amazing product!", "Highly recommend!", "Life-changing!".
- Initials only, or no name, so they look invented.
- Quotes edited into polished marketing copy that no customer would write.
- All the quotes say the same thing.
- A wall of testimonials at the bottom, far from the claims they could support.
- Fake, paid or incentivised reviews presented as organic. Treat this as a serious problem.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts (for example quotes that look fake).
- **1:** present but weak: generic praise, thin attribution, or clustered where nobody reads them.
- **2:** does its job: specific quotes from identifiable people like the reader, placed near the claims.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: quotes answer the reader's main doubts with concrete results in the customer's own voice.

## Fixes to suggest
- Ask customers one specific question ("What changed in your first month?") and use the answer as given.
- Add full attribution: name, role or city, photo, with permission.
- Put each testimonial next to the claim or objection it answers.
- Cut duplicates. Keep the one that says it most specifically.
- Lead with the result in a short bold pull-quote, then the full quote.

## Example
**Weak (Ledgerly):**
> "Great app, highly recommend!" (J.D.)

**Stronger (Ledgerly):**
> "My Q3 VAT return took 20 minutes. Last year it took me a whole Sunday and a call to my accountant."
> Sara M., freelance translator, Ghent

Why it works: a specific before and after from a named person the target freelancer recognises.

**Facts only the writer has:** real customer quotes, names, roles, photos, permission to publish, the results they reported.
