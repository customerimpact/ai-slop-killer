# Proof signals

**Its job:** give the reader quick, at-a-glance evidence that others already buy and trust this product, so it feels safe and established.

**Needed on:** landing pages, sales pages, product pages and funnel steps, usually just under the headline (a short strip) and again near the call to action. Ads can carry one signal (a rating or a customer count). Marketing emails can use one line. Brand-new products without real signals should skip it rather than fake it.

## What good looks like
- Signals are real and current: customer counts, ratings with the number of reviews, client logos, press mentions, awards.
- They're relevant to this reader: logos from the same sector, ratings from a platform the reader knows.
- Numbers are specific ("4.7 from 1,318 reviews", "2,400 freelancers") and dated if they change.
- They sit where doubt shows up: under the headline, next to the price, by the button.
- They're compact. A strip or a line, not a whole section.
- They link or point to where the reader can verify them.

## What usually goes wrong
- Vague claims: "trusted by thousands", "as seen in the media".
- Logos of companies that once tried a free account, or that the reader won't recognise.
- A star rating with no review count or source.
- Out-of-date numbers that make the product look stalled.
- Press logos for a passing mention or a paid placement.
- Signals placed so low on the page that nobody sees them.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts (for example signals that look fake).
- **1:** present but weak: vague, unsourced or irrelevant to this reader.
- **2:** does its job: specific, checkable signals relevant to the reader, placed near the headline and the call to action.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the signals tell the reader "people like you already chose this", instantly and believably.

## Fixes to suggest
- Replace "thousands" with the real count and the date.
- Add the review count and the platform to every rating.
- Show logos from the reader's own sector first. Drop the rest.
- Put a one-line proof strip under the headline and repeat one signal by the button.
- If there are no real signals yet, cut the section and lean on a guarantee or a demo.

## Example
**Weak (Harbor Roast):**
> Loved by coffee fans everywhere! As seen in the press. 5 stars!

**Stronger (Harbor Roast):**
> 3,100 subscribers in Belgium and the Netherlands. Rated 4.8 from 940 reviews. Average subscriber has stayed 14 months.

Why it works: specific, checkable numbers tell the reader people stay, which answers the subscription worry.

**Facts only the writer has:** real customer counts, ratings and review counts with sources, client logos with permission, press links, dates.
