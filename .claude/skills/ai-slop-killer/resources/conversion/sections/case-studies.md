# Case studies

**Its job:** show one customer's full journey, from problem to result, in enough detail that a similar buyer believes the same path will work for them.

**Needed on:** B2B sales pages, landing pages for high-ticket offers, demo-led funnels and webinar or VSL scripts. Usually after the solution, or as a linked page from near the call to action. Consumer products under a modest price can skip it and use testimonials instead.

## What good looks like
- The customer is named and looks like the target buyer (size, sector, role, situation).
- It covers the before (the problem and its cost), what they did (setup, time, effort) and the after (measured results).
- Results are numbers with a time frame: hours saved per week, costs cut, sales up, over how many months.
- The customer speaks in their own words at least once.
- It admits friction: what was hard, how long setup took, what they'd do differently.
- A short summary up top lets a busy reader get the result in five seconds.

## What usually goes wrong
- A success story with no numbers, or numbers with no time frame or baseline.
- The customer is anonymous ("a leading restaurant group").
- It reads like a press release about the seller, not the customer's problem.
- Every case is a huge customer the target buyer can't relate to.
- Results credited to the product that clearly came from other changes.
- Too long, with the result buried at the bottom.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts.
- **1:** present but weak: anonymous, number-free or a disguised feature pitch.
- **2:** does its job: a named, relevant customer with before, process and measured after.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reader sees a customer just like them get a specific result on a believable timeline, and wants the same.

## Fixes to suggest
- Add a three-line summary at the top: who, problem, result with numbers.
- Get the baseline and the time frame for every result.
- Pick cases that match the main buyer segment. Add a smaller one if all cases are big names.
- Include one honest line about what was hard.
- Quote the customer directly on the result.

## Example
**Weak (ShiftDesk):**
> A leading restaurant group improved efficiency and staff satisfaction with ShiftDesk.

**Stronger (ShiftDesk):**
> **Four restaurants, 85 staff, rota time down from 6 hours to 1 a week.**
> Before ShiftDesk, the operations manager built rotas in a spreadsheet every Sunday night. Setup took two weeks, mostly importing staff availability. After three months, last-minute no-shows had fallen from about nine a month to three.
> "I got my Sunday evenings back." Operations manager, restaurant group

Why it works: a relevant buyer, a measured before and after, a time frame and an honest setup cost.

**Facts only the writer has:** customer name and permission, baseline numbers, results, time frames, setup effort, real quotes.
