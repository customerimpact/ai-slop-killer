# Tiers and plans

**Its job:** help each reader pick the right plan fast, without feeling tricked or stuck.

**Needed on:** pricing pages and SaaS or subscription product pages, and on sales pages that sell more than one package. Funnel steps may show tiers at checkout. Skip it when there's one offer; don't invent tiers just to have them.

## What good looks like
- Each tier is named for who it's for or what it does, so the reader can find themselves in one line.
- Differences between tiers are few, clear and meaningful. The reader sees what changes when they move up.
- One tier is marked as the usual choice, with a reason, not just a "popular" badge.
- Limits that matter (users, locations, volume, support) are stated in numbers.
- Upgrading and downgrading terms are clear, including what happens to data or access.

## What usually goes wrong
- Too many tiers, or tiers that differ in tiny ways, which stalls the decision.
- Long feature grids where most rows are the same across every column.
- A decoy tier so obviously bad it insults the reader.
- Names like Basic, Pro, Premium that say nothing about fit.
- Key limits hidden in footnotes, then discovered after signing up.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** tiers confuse the choice or hide limits that will bite later.
- **1:** present but weak: generic names, a wall of identical rows, no guidance on which to pick.
- **2:** does its job: few tiers, clear differences, limits in numbers, a recommended choice.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reader picks in seconds because each tier describes their situation, and moving up feels like a natural next step.

## Fixes to suggest
- Cut to the fewest tiers that cover real buyer types, usually two or three.
- Rename each tier after who it's for, or add a one-line "for…" under each name.
- Show only the rows that differ, and move shared features to a single line above the grid.
- Mark the recommended tier and say in a few words why most buyers pick it.
- State the limits that trigger an upgrade, in numbers, in the grid itself.

## Example
**Weak (ShiftDesk):**
> Basic | Pro | Enterprise. 40 features compared below. Contact us for pricing.

**Stronger (ShiftDesk):**
> **One kitchen:** a single location, up to 30 staff.
> **Group:** 2 to 10 locations, shared staff across sites, labour cost by site. Most groups start here.
> **Large group:** 10+ locations, custom roles and payroll export.
> Every plan includes the mobile app and shift-swap approvals. Book a demo to get the price for your group.

Why it works: owners see their own size in a tier name, the differences are the ones they ask about, and shared basics stop cluttering the choice.

**Facts only the writer has:** the real tiers, limits, prices or price basis, which tier most buyers choose, and upgrade terms.
