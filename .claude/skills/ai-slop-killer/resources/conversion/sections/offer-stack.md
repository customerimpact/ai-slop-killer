# Offer stack

**Its job:** make the reader see exactly what they get, and feel that the whole is worth clearly more than the price.

**Needed on:** sales pages, webinar and VSL scripts, and checkout funnel steps, placed just before the price is revealed and often repeated near the final ask. Product pages need a short "what's included". Ads and emails can skip it or give a one-line version.

## What good looks like
- Every item is listed by name, with what it does for the buyer, not just what it is.
- The core product comes first and gets the most weight. Extras support it.
- Quantities and formats are specific: number of lessons, hours of video, calls per month, how long access lasts.
- Any value attached to an item is defensible (a real standalone price, or a clear basis for the figure).
- The stack is short enough to scan. A reader can repeat back what's in it.

## What usually goes wrong
- A pile of filler items (checklists, "templates", a private group) added to look big.
- Made-up "value" figures that add up to a ridiculous total nobody believes.
- Items described by format only ("Module 4: 6 videos") with no outcome attached.
- The main product gets buried under the extras.
- The stack on the sales page doesn't match what checkout or the welcome email delivers.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** the reader can't tell what they'd get, or the stack is inflated to the point of hurting trust.
- **1:** present but weak: a list of formats and file names, no outcomes, or invented value figures.
- **2:** does its job: complete, specific, each item tied to a benefit, core product leading.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: every item answers a need raised earlier on the page, values are credible, and the total makes the price feel small without any stretching.

## Fixes to suggest
- For each item, add one line starting with what the buyer can do once they have it.
- Remove any item you'd be embarrassed to have a buyer open first.
- Replace invented values with real standalone prices, or drop the values altogether.
- Put the core product first and give it at least as many words as all extras combined.
- Check the stack against the delivery: if it's listed, it must arrive.

## Example
**Weak (Clay Weekends):**
> You get: 8 modules, bonus PDFs, community access, and more! Total value €2,000+.

**Stronger (Clay Weekends):**
> - 8 weekend lessons, filmed over the wheel, from centring your first lump to trimming a bowl you'll use.
> - A printable kit list, so you buy the right clay and tools once.
> - Lifetime access, so you can rewatch the trimming lesson as often as your bowls need it.
>
> All for €149.

Why it works: each item names a result the beginner wants, nothing is padded, and the price lands against things the reader can picture.

**Facts only the writer has:** the real list of what's delivered, lesson counts and lengths, any standalone prices that back a stated value.
