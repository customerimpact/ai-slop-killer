# Angle

**Its job:** give the whole piece one central idea that makes this offer feel new and relevant, so every section points the same way.

**Needed on:** all copy types, but it's not a block of its own. You judge it across the piece. It should be visible in the headline, carried by the opening and paid off by the mechanism or the offer. Long sales pages and VSL scripts need it most. Short ads and emails need it too, since they have room for only one idea. Check for it even when there's no single place it lives.

## What good looks like
- You can state it in one sentence after reading the piece ("Freelancers lose money because they do books in panic mode once a year").
- It's a fresh way to see the problem or the solution, not just "our product is good".
- It suits where the reader is: problem-aware readers get a new view of the problem; solution-aware readers get a new reason this solution is different.
- The headline, opening, mechanism and close all reinforce it.
- It's true and the product can back it up.
- It's specific to this offer. A competitor couldn't paste it onto their page.

## What usually goes wrong
- No angle at all: a list of features and benefits with no idea tying them together.
- Several angles competing, so the reader doesn't know what to remember.
- The angle shows up in the headline and then vanishes.
- It's a generic category promise ("save time", "get fit") that every rival also makes.
- It's an attention trick that the product can't support, so the page loses trust halfway down.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing, or the piece is pulled in several directions and confuses the reader.
- **1:** present but weak: an idea exists but it's generic, or it fades after the first screen.
- **2:** does its job: one clear idea, specific to the offer, carried through most sections.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reader sees the problem differently after reading, and that view leads straight to this product.

## Fixes to suggest
- Write the angle in one sentence at the top of the draft. Cut or rewrite any section that doesn't serve it.
- Pick the single strongest angle and move the others to future ads or emails.
- Make it something the reader hasn't heard ten times: a cause they missed, a cost they didn't count, a method they haven't tried.
- Echo it in the close and the call to action, not only the headline.
- Check the product really delivers on it. If not, choose a different angle.

## Example
Weak (ShiftDesk):
> ShiftDesk is a powerful, easy-to-use scheduling tool with lots of great features for restaurants.

Stronger:
> The rota takes you an hour. The swaps take your whole week: the 11pm texts when someone can't make Saturday. ShiftDesk is built around the swap.

The stronger one gives the reader a new way to see their pain, and the product is the natural answer to it.

**Facts only the writer has:** what customers say is their real pain, how the product actually handles it, and any data showing it (for example, share of shifts swapped).
