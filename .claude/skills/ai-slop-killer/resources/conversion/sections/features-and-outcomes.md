# Features and outcomes

**Its job:** show the reader what the product does and what each part changes for them, so the offer feels concrete and worth the price.

**Needed on:** sales pages, landing pages and product pages, usually after the problem and the mechanism and before the offer. Funnel steps and webinar or VSL scripts need a short version. Ads and marketing emails can carry one or two outcomes and skip the full list.

## What good looks like
- Each feature is tied to a result the reader cares about, in their words ("so you can send the invoice before you leave the client's office").
- Outcomes are specific: time saved, money kept, a task that disappears, a moment that gets easier.
- The list is ordered by what matters most to the buyer, not by how the product was built.
- Claims stay believable. Where an outcome is large, a number, a screenshot or proof sits nearby.
- The section is easy to scan: short lines, one idea per line, the outcome in bold or first.
- Features the reader has never heard of get one line of plain explanation.

## What usually goes wrong
- A spec sheet: features listed with no outcome attached ("Multi-currency support. Bank sync. Reports.").
- Outcomes so vague they could sit on any page ("save time", "work smarter", "grow your business").
- Too many items. Twenty equal bullets bury the three that would sell.
- Internal jargon and product names for features the reader can't picture.
- Outcomes promised that the product can't deliver on its own, which makes the reader distrust the rest.
- The same benefit repeated in five different phrasings.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts.
- **1:** present but weak: features with no outcomes, or outcomes too generic to picture.
- **2:** does its job: most features tie to a clear, believable outcome, and the list is ordered and scannable.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: the reader sees their own week getting better, line by line, with proof behind the biggest claims.

## Fixes to suggest
- For every feature, finish the sentence "so you can..." and keep the half that matters to the buyer.
- Cut the list to the five to seven items buyers ask about most. Move the rest to a "full feature list" link.
- Replace each vague outcome with a moment from the reader's day.
- Reorder by buyer priority. Ask sales or support which questions come up first.
- Put proof (a number, a screenshot, a short quote) next to the boldest outcome.

## Example
**Weak (Ledgerly):**
> Bank sync. Invoice templates. Expense categories. VAT reports. Save time on admin.

**Stronger (Ledgerly):**
> **Invoices go out before you leave the client's office.** Pick a template, add hours, send from your phone.
> **Your VAT return is ready when the quarter ends.** Expenses sort themselves as your bank feeds in.
> **You know what you can spend.** The tax you'll owe is set aside on screen, every month.

Why it works: each line starts with a change the freelancer can picture, then names the feature that causes it.

**Facts only the writer has:** which features buyers use most, real time-saved figures, supported banks and countries, screenshots.
