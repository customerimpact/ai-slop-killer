# How it works

**Its job:** show the reader the few steps from buying to getting the result, so it feels easy and they can picture doing it.

**Needed on:** landing pages, product pages and sales pages, usually after the mechanism or the product overview. Software, services, subscriptions and courses need it most. Webinar and VSL scripts walk through it before the offer. Opt-in pages can use a short version (what happens after you sign up). Ads and emails can skip it or reduce it to one line.

## What good looks like
- Three to five steps, numbered, each starting with a verb.
- The first step is easy and immediate (sign up, book, pick a plan).
- Each step says what the reader does and what they get.
- It's honest about effort and time ("about 10 minutes", "three sessions a week").
- The last step is the result, in the reader's terms.
- Visuals or screenshots, where available, match each step.

## What usually goes wrong
- Too many steps, so it looks like work.
- Steps that describe the company's process instead of the reader's ("We analyse your data").
- Vague steps ("Get started", "See results") that give no picture.
- Effort is hidden, then surprises the reader later and causes refunds or churn.
- It's missing, and the reader can't tell what happens after they click buy.

## Scoring (0–3)
- **0:** missing where it's needed, or it actively hurts (confusing or misleading about effort).
- **1:** present but weak: vague steps, too many steps, or company-side language.
- **2:** does its job: a short, clear sequence from start to result, written from the reader's side.
- **3:** a reason to buy on its own: it makes getting started look so simple that the main objection (effort) goes away.

## Fixes to suggest
- Cut to the three to five steps that matter. Merge or drop the rest.
- Start each step with what the reader does, then what they get.
- Add real time and effort figures to the steps.
- Make the final step the outcome, not "enjoy".
- Add a screenshot or image for each step if the product is visual.

## Example
Weak (ShiftDesk):
> 1. Onboarding 2. Configuration 3. Optimisation 4. Success

Stronger:
> 1. Book a 20-minute demo and we'll import your current rota.
> 2. Your staff get an app invite and set their availability.
> 3. Publish next week's schedule in one click. Swaps get approved from your phone.

The stronger one says what the manager does at each step and how long it takes, so switching feels manageable.

**Facts only the writer has:** real setup time, what's imported for the customer, and what staff actually have to do.
